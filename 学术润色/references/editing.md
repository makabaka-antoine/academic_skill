# Language and terminology

## Correct the defect where it occurs

- For agreement, articles, countability, punctuation and collocations, change the affected expression and check the resulting subject, predicate and modifier attachment. A dangling modifier must name its intended actor only when the source identifies it.
- Split a sentence at a genuine proposition boundary. Repeat a condition or noun phrase when needed to keep its scope clear. Read pronouns, comparison complements and relative clauses aloud in context. Keep the evidence with the proposition it supports.
- Remove empty emphasis and repeated announcements. Preserve meaningful contrast, enumeration, uncertainty, statistical qualifiers, domain terminology and any useful figure or section signpost. Use the source or requested house style to decide spelling, tense, first person, punctuation and technical formatting.
- Reread both the edited sentence and its neighbors for local prose. A grammatically corrected sentence can still have an awkward repeated common word or a bare “difference” with two plausible comparisons. Clarify using terms and relationships already established. Repeat a formal name where it is needed to retain its referent.
- In Chinese, check the reach of 修饰语, 否定词, 所有/部分, and numbers with units. Restore the genuine subject if an implicit subject is ambiguous. Keep useful particles and connective words. In English, put an identifiable subject before a long modifier and make each comparison parallel. A method the study performed normally uses the past tense. Definitions and claims about the text may use the present.
- Let genre determine emphasis. For an abstract, make the result legible within the actual evidence and length allowance. For a thesis, preserve procedural detail. For a grant proposal, retain the distinction between plans, assumptions and completed work. In a response letter, distinguish the reviewer's quoted words, the authors' reply, and changes already made.

## Terms across a draft

Start with the case or author's approved glossary, style instructions and terminology in the authoritative text. Treat proposals based on dominant usage as candidates until the relevant definitions and uses establish equivalence. Preserve distinct constructs that happen to use similar words. When one spelling is assigned two different meanings, keep ambiguous instances and report both mappings and the affected locations. Continue unambiguous edits.

For multiple sections, track each approved term, abbreviation and where it is first defined in the relevant reading unit. Check abstract, main text, and standalone captions separately when the supplied format requires independent definitions. Expand only an abbreviation whose full form is provided or unambiguous in the source. Keep proper names, data identifiers, variable names and established technical compounds stable. ## Chinese and English alignment

Translate propositions, then arrange them naturally for the target reader. Decide who did what, which groups are compared, how the result is qualified, and where each citation applies before changing word order. Use supplied equivalences, and keep a plausible untranslated term with an author query if translation would choose between scientific meanings. Keep numeric precision and the distinction between percent and percentage points. Maintain the hierarchy of condition, exception and conclusion. Render significant, independent, validated and similar words at the strength the source assigns them.

For bilingual output, give each aligned unit the same source ID and pair both languages at the requested granularity. Back-check values, units, quantified groups, directional verbs, scope, uncertainty and citation/object attachment from the two deliverable versions, not from memory. A coherent paragraph may use more than one sentence on one side. Mixed-language names, formulae and quotations can remain in their original language.
