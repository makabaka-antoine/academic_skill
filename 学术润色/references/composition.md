# Evidence-based drafting and citation work

1. Read the intended deliverable, audience, section type and length. Locate the authoritative method, results and limitations. Resolve genuine conflicts where authority is explicit. Otherwise report them and leave uncertain information out of new prose or bound it to what the record establishes.
2. List the propositions the deliverable needs. For each, capture the source passage or table cell, scope and role. Map every numerical result to its group, comparator, outcome and denominator before wording it. Confirm the interval type and whether percentages are relative changes, rates, or differences in percentage points. Keep negative results and design limitations where they govern interpretation.
3. Draft the complete requested text. Abstracts usually benefit from a specific question, design or comparison, principal result and bounded interpretation. Follow any supplied structure. Explain the logical link between adjacent sentences. Use the space budget for essential numbers and conditions. For longer edits, arrange details around the requested argument while keeping citations attached. Fix repeated common-word stems, jumping antecedents and buried comparisons after content is stable.
4. Map substantive output sentences to exact support, with retained claims or interpretive boundaries labeled. Check final sentence boundaries after citation or format changes. A citation key points somewhere. Verification needs an actually read source that supports the cited claim.

For citation tasks, inventory the in-text citations and supplied bibliography first. Match exact keys or a confirmed author, year and title. Follow the requested ordering and grouping, including repeated citations reusing their source number. Update prose and bibliography together and check for orphan keys, duplicate entries, changed links and changed attachment. If identifying metadata is unavailable, retain an identifiable pointer or supply an honest partial entry and report the missing field outside manuscript prose.
